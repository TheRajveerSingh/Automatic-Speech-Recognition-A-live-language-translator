⏱Where the time went

1Cleaning the audio our DSP front-end · asr/audio.py

Microphones capture more than your voice: an electrical offset (DC), low rumble from desks and fans, and long silences that make Whisper "hallucinate" words. Before any AI runs, we clean the signal with classic signal processing.

Waveform: raw vs cleaned

Each pixel column shows the loudest and quietest sample in that slice of time. Grey areas are the silence we trim away.

raw input after filter + normalisation trimmed

Frequency content & the high-pass filter

Average energy at each frequency (Welch's method). A 4th-order Butterworth filter removes everything below Hz - below the lowest human voice - without touching speech.

before after filter gain

Voice activity detection (VAD)

The audio is cut into ms frames and each frame's loudness is computed. Frames louder than the adaptive threshold count as speech.

speech frame silence threshold noise floor
loudness(frame) = 20·log₁₀( √(mean(x²)) ) dBFS noise floor = 10th percentile of frame loudness threshold = max(noise floor + 10 dB, −55 dB) gain = min(−1 dBFS − peak, +30 dB)

2Turning sound into a picture: the log-Mel spectrogram asr/features.py

Neural networks don't hear - they see numbers. Whisper converts audio into a log-Mel spectrogram: a picture of which pitches are present at each moment, warped to match how human ears perceive pitch. We compute it ourselves, step by step, and check it against Whisper's own code.

    One frame's journey

    Here is the single loudest 25 ms slice of your recording going through each step.

    The full spectrogram - what Whisper actually sees

    Time runs left → right (one column every 10 ms), pitch runs bottom → top, brightness = energy. During decoding, the words Whisper found are placed at the times it heard them.

    3Which language is this?

    Before writing anything, Whisper's decoder predicts a special language token from the first 30 s of audio. It scores every one of its languages; these are the top candidates (probabilities sum to 100%).

    4Decoding: sound → tokens → words

    Whisper's encoder turns the spectrogram into 1,500 feature vectors. The decoder then writes text one token at a time (a token is a word or word-piece from a vocabulary of ). Beam search keeps the most likely partial sentences alive at every step and picks the best complete one. replay the animation re-plays the real tokens and timings.

        How this summary was made

        The transcript is too long for a small model to read well in one go, so it's summarised in two rounds (map-reduce): first each chunk of a few minutes gets its own notes, title and action items; then the model reads only those notes to write the final summary.
